# Terms, reuse, and due-diligence boundaries

## Website terms

Heritage New Zealand Pouhere Taonga publishes its website terms at:

- <https://www.heritage.org.nz/terms-and-conditions>
- <https://www.heritage.org.nz/about-this-site>

The terms assert copyright in website content, including Heritage List information, and limit reproduction without permission except where New Zealand law permits uses such as private study, research, review, or education. Images and third-party material can carry separate rights.

This skill therefore:

- downloads the official export only to answer the current read query;
- returns a bounded set of factual fields;
- omits `LegalDescription` and `ExtentOfListEntry` from user-facing output;
- does not bundle, cache, mirror, or republish the full export;
- uses a wholly synthetic deterministic fixture; and
- links users back to the official source for primary-detail review.

Do not use this tool to create a public mirror or bulk commercial republication. Seek permission from Heritage New Zealand Pouhere Taonga where the intended reuse is outside a lawful exception or the source's stated permissions.

## Property and legal limitations

The CSV is useful discovery evidence, not a complete legal/property due-diligence product. In particular:

1. A name or address search can miss an entry because names, boundaries, addresses, and council labels change.
2. The export address is not a property-title or cadastral identifier.
3. List status does not by itself answer ownership, access, resource-consent, building-consent, district-plan, covenant, wāhi tapu, or archaeological-authority questions.
4. A place can have archaeological significance or legal obligations even when this List search has no match.
5. The CSV can lag the detailed page or another primary record.

For a decision with legal, planning, purchase, development, demolition, subdivision, earthworks, or access consequences, check the current detailed List record, council planning/LIM material, title and survey records, and obtain advice from Heritage New Zealand Pouhere Taonga and qualified professionals.

## Responsible quoting

Prefer list number, name, type, status, council, address, dates, and the source link. Quote only the minimum source text needed for the task. Do not copy List narratives, images, legal descriptions, or extent text into reports by default.
